# Artifact

A digital forensic artifact is a data trace on a system or storage medium that was created by the operating system, an application or an activity of the user and allows conclusions about an event or an action.

Artifacts are mostly created as a by-product of normal operation, e.g. for performance optimisation, troubleshooting or convenience, and not for the purpose of documenting activities. Examples on Windows are Prefetch files, which indicate the execution of programs, or ShellBags, which indicate accessed folders.

From a digital forensics perspective, an artifact is only meaningful in the context of how it was created: it must be checked who or what created it, when and why, and what it proves or does not prove. Individual artifacts are therefore often compared with other artifacts to corroborate a statement.
